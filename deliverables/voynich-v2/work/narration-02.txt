Begin with the object, not the theories. The Voynich Manuscript is a small parchment codex, about nine by six inches. Radiocarbon analysis of four samples dated its animal-skin pages to between 1404 and 1438. Its inks and pigments use materials compatible with historical manuscript production.

Important distinction: radiocarbon dates the parchment, not each stroke of ink. By itself, it cannot prove the exact day the writing was added. But together with the book's physical construction, pigments, wear, and later ownership record, the evidence strongly supports a genuinely medieval codex.

Then paleographer Lisa Fagin Davis examined tiny differences in the handwriting: how glyphs lean, connect, and repeat. Her conclusion was not one scribe, but five.

That changes the scale of the mystery. Several people seem to have learned the same unfamiliar writing system and coordinated a large illustrated book. We still do not know what they wrote. But we know this was organized work.
